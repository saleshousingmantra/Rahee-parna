Choosing the Right Part of Pune for Your Home: Is Raahee Parna in Moshi the Answer?

By Raahee Corp · 27 October 2026 · 3 min read

Pune is one of India's most liveable cities, with strong education, a thriving job market and a pleasant climate. But the city is large, and choosing the right area for your home can feel overwhelming. This guide gives you a simple checklist to decide where to buy and shows how Raahee Parna in Dudulgaon, near Moshi, measures up.

Start with your daily routine

The best location is the one that fits your life. Write down where you and your family spend most of your time: offices, schools, hospitals, parents' homes and favourite markets. A slightly higher price in a well connected area often saves hours each week, while a quieter location can improve quality of life if your commute is manageable. Be honest about which of the two matters more to your household.

Compare the main parts of Pune

Pune is not one market but several. Established central neighbourhoods offer convenience and heritage but tend to be costlier and more crowded. IT-focused areas such as Hinjewadi and Kharadi suit those working in technology. The Pimpri Chinchwad (PCMC) belt, which includes Moshi, Charholi and Dudulgaon, offers more space and greenery along with access to industrial, educational and commercial hubs. Each has trade-offs, so match the area to your priorities.

A checklist for choosing your home

  • Budget: decide your total comfortable outlay, including stamp duty, registration and GST
  • Commute: test the real travel time to work at peak hours
  • Social infrastructure: schools, hospitals, markets and colleges within 15 minutes
  • Amenities: facilities your family will actually use
  • Developer and legal checks: MahaRERA registration and track record
  • Future needs: space for a growing family or a work-from-home room

Common mistakes to avoid

Many buyers decide on price alone and ignore the daily commute, or fall for a long amenity list without checking whether the facilities will be maintained. Others skip the legal checks. Take your time, compare projects on the same basis, and remember that the cheapest option is not always the best value. Speak to current residents where possible, and ask the developer for clear answers on timelines, charges and approvals.

Make the decision with confidence

Shortlist two or three projects, visit each at different times of day, and compare carpet area, amenities and total cost side by side.
